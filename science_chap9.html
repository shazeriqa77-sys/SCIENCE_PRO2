🔥 Chapter 9: Heat

Master the concepts of heat and temperature, heat flow, thermal equilibrium, expansion and contraction of matter, and heat absorption and radiation.

🌡️ 9.1 Temperature and Heat

Understanding the difference between heat energy and temperature.

  • Heat: A form of energy that flows from a hotter region to a colder region.
    - S.I. Unit: Joule (J).
    - Depends on the type of material, mass, and temperature of the object.
  • Temperature: A measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object.
    - S.I. Unit: Kelvin (K) (commonly measured in Degree Celsius, °C using a thermometer).

🌊 9.2 Heat Flow and Thermal Equilibrium

Three methods of heat transfer and natural phenomena.

  • Conduction: Heat transfer through solids by direct vibration and collision of particles from hot to cold ends.
  • Convection: Heat transfer in fluids (liquids and gases) through movement of heated fluid rising and cooler fluid sinking (e.g., sea breeze during the day and land breeze at night).
  • Radiation: Heat transfer in the form of electromagnetic waves without requiring any medium (can travel through a vacuum, e.g., heat from the Sun).
  • Thermal Equilibrium: Achieved when two objects in thermal contact reach the same temperature and there is no net heat flow between them.

📏 9.3 Expansion and Contraction of Matter

How heating and cooling affect the volume of solids, liquids, and gases.

  • Principle: When heated, particles vibrate faster and move further apart, causing matter to expand. When cooled, particles move closer together, causing matter to contract (mass remains constant).
  • Daily Applications:
    - Bimetallic strip: Used in fire alarms and thermostats because different metals expand at different rates.
    - Gaps in railway tracks & bridges: Prevent buckling during hot weather.
    - Mercury in thermometers: Expands and contracts uniformly with temperature changes.

☀️ 9.4 Heat Absorption and Emission

How surface color and texture affect heat radiation.

  • Dark, Dull Surfaces: Good absorbers and good radiators (emitters) of heat (e.g., black cooling fins at the back of a refrigerator).
  • White, Shiny Surfaces: Poor absorbers and poor radiators of heat, but good reflectors of heat (e.g., silver-painted fuel tanks, white buildings in hot climates).
  • Green Building Concept: Uses thermal insulation, natural ventilation, and reflective roofs to reduce heat gain and save energy.

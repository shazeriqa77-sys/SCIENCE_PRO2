✨ Chapter 11: Stars and Galaxies in the Universe

Explore the vastness of the universe, the characteristics of stars and galaxies, and the life cycle of stars.

🌌 11.1 Stars and Galaxies

The universe consists of millions of galaxies, and each galaxy contains billions of stars.

  • Universe: Comprises everything that exists in space, including all matter, energy, planets, stars, and galaxies.
  • Galaxy: A vast system containing millions to billions of stars, gas, and dust held together by gravity.
  • Types of Galaxies:
    - Spiral Galaxy: Has spiral arms (e.g., Milky Way and Andromeda).
    - Elliptical Galaxy: Round or oval shape with older stars.
    - Irregular Galaxy: No definite shape, containing young and bright stars.
  • The Milky Way: A large spiral galaxy where our Solar System is located.

⭐ 11.2 The Sun and Characteristics of Stars

Stars are celestial bodies made of hot, glowing gas that produce their own heat and light through nuclear fusion.

  • Characteristics of Stars: Size, color, surface temperature, and distance from Earth.
    - Blue Stars: Extremely hot (surface temperature > 25,000°C).
    - Red Stars: Cooler surface temperature (around 3,000°C).
    - Yellow Stars: Moderate temperature, like our Sun (~6,000°C).
  • Light-Year: The distance that light travels in one year (approximately 9.5 trillion kilometres), used to measure vast distances in space.

🌠 11.3 Life Cycle of a Star

Stars are born, evolve over billions of years, and eventually die depending on their initial mass.

  • Birth: Begins in a Nebula (a giant cloud of gas and dust) where gravity pulls particles together to form a Protostar.
  • Main Sequence Star: Nuclear fusion begins, releasing massive amounts of energy (our Sun is currently in this stage).
  • Aging & Death:
    - Average-sized stars: Expand into a Red Giant, cast off outer layers into a Planetary Nebula, and shrink into a White Dwarf.
    - Massive stars: Expand into a Supergiant, explode in a brilliant Supernova, and collapse into a dense Neutron Star or a Black Hole.

🔭 11.4 Exploration of the Universe

How humanity studies and understands the universe beyond Earth.

  • Astronomical Instruments: Optical telescopes, radio telescopes, and space telescopes (like the Hubble and James Webb Space Telescopes) allow scientists to view distant cosmic objects.
  • Space Probes: Unmanned spacecraft sent to explore planets, moons, and outer space without risking human lives.
